01 / UNDERSTAND

What is Phishing?

Understand the threat before learning how to defend against it.

Phishing is a cyber attack in which criminals impersonate trusted people or organizations to trick users into revealing sensitive information, clicking harmful links or downloading malicious files.

Why do attackers use phishing?

  • Steal usernames and passwords.
  • Obtain banking and financial information.
  • Gain unauthorized access to accounts.
  • Commit identity theft and financial fraud.
  • Access confidential organizational information.

03 / INVESTIGATE

How to Identify Phishing

Learn to recognize suspicious messages, fake login pages and fraudulent websites.

📨

Suspicious Sender

Look for unknown addresses, misspellings and domains that imitate genuine organizations.

⏰

Urgent Messages

Be cautious of messages threatening account closure or demanding immediate action.

🔗

Unexpected Links

Check the actual destination of a link before opening it. Avoid unexpected attachments.

Example: Analyze a Suspicious Email

From: Account Support

Subject: URGENT! Your account will be blocked.

Dear Customer,

We detected unusual activity in your account. Verify your password and OTP immediately to prevent account suspension.

Click here: account-check.example.invalid

⚠ Warning Signs

  • Creates fear and urgency.
  • Requests sensitive information.
  • Uses an unverified address.
  • Uses a generic greeting.
This is a fictional training example. Never enter real passwords, banking details or OTPs into unverified pages.

How to Identify Fake Login Pages

  • Check the exact website domain.
  • Look for spelling mistakes or extra characters in URLs.
  • Be careful with unexpected login requests.
  • Never enter credentials through unknown links.
  • Use official apps or saved bookmarks to visit important services.

How to Identify Fraudulent Websites

  • Unusual domain names.
  • Unexpected redirects and pop-ups.
  • Unusual payment or personal information requests.
  • Offers that seem too good to be true.
HTTPS and a padlock do not guarantee that a website is genuine. Always verify the exact domain and organization.

05 / REAL WORLD

Real-Life Case Study

Learn how business email fraud can cause serious financial damage.

Business Email Compromise – Evaldas Rimasauskas Case

Evaldas Rimasauskas used a company name resembling that of a legitimate hardware supplier to send fraudulent business emails to employees of two multinational technology companies.

The emails directed payments to bank accounts controlled by the criminal. The U.S. Department of Justice reported that the scheme involved more than $100 million in fraudulent transfers. Rimasauskas pleaded guilty in 2019.

Lessons Learned

  • Verify payment requests independently.
  • Check supplier details carefully.
  • Do not trust an email only because its name looks familiar.
  • Use multiple approval steps for large payments.
  • Report suspicious financial instructions.

Source: U.S. Department of Justice

06 / STAY SAFE

Practical Security Tips

Build safe digital habits and reduce your risk of phishing.

🔐

Strong Passwords

Use strong, unique passwords for different accounts.

🛡️

Enable MFA

Use multi-factor authentication to add another layer of security.

🔎

Verify First

Check unexpected messages through an official communication channel.

What to Do If You Click a Phishing Link

  1. Stop interacting with the suspicious page.
  2. Do not enter additional information.
  3. If you shared a password, change it from a trusted device.
  4. Contact your bank if financial information was shared.
  5. Enable MFA and review account activity.
  6. Report the incident to the relevant organization.
Remember: Pause, inspect and verify before clicking an unexpected link.
